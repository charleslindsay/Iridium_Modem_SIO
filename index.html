Features

Power
  • Wide input voltage range (5 – 20 V)
  • Jumpers to selectively disable devices for low-power operation
Peripherals
  • Dual RS-232 ports for parallel communication and position data
Satellite Module
  • Iridium Core 9523N
    • RUDICS | SBD protocol
GNSS Module
  • Quectel L96-M33
    • Multi-constellation tracking
    • Flash memory
    • LNA
  • Cold start < 45 s | warm start < 20 s
  • Active or passive GPS antenna

Design Challenges

Mechanical Constraint

Board must mount to a metal chassis housed inside a vacuum-sealed pressure vessel. This must withstand subsurface pressure at 100 m to survive initial buoy deployment.

RF Problem
  • Interference: Pressure vessel diameter < λ/2 results in antenna coupling and RF power leakage into GNSS front-end.
  • Directionality: Unpredictable wave motion means antenna orientation changes constantly.
Thermal Problem
  • Heat dissipation: Vacuum-sealed means heat will only transfer radiatively. During tropical buoy deployments with long uplink connect times, there is a risk of exceeding the Iridium module's max operational temperature.
Solutions
  • RF switch: Protects GNSS front-end during Iridium transmission with 12 dB isolation between the external antenna and module. Low insertion loss of 0.7 dB ensures a passive antenna is still compatible with the system.
  • Vertical wide-beamwidth antennas: The side-lobe gain at the maximum tilt expected on deployed buoys is sufficient to close the link budget for both GNSS and Iridium. Antennas are mounted side-by-side on SMA bulkheads for easy installation.
  • Thermal strap: Copper pads with exposed soldermask were added to the top and bottom layers. Vias transfer heat from all layers to these pads, placed near the highest-power regulator and the Iridium module — the two components expected to dissipate the most heat.
